# Normative EU privacy, cookie and session requirements — v3.1

**Scope and legal gate:** Applies when targeting EU/EEA users. These are engineering requirements, **not a claim of legal certification**. Before allowing public signups or payments, appoint a human controller/merchant, determine controller/processor arrangements among platform, instructors, GitHub, Firebase, Cloudflare, video hosts, payment processor, and obtain counsel/privacy review appropriate for Bulgaria and target countries. Preserve evidence of review; code generators must not fabricate legal identity or controller contact fields.

## PRIV requirements

- PRIV-001 Data mapping and lawful basis. Maintain a data inventory: identity (Firebase UID/email); real name for certificate; enrollment, coursework, feedback and grades; GitHub repo ID, subscription/access rights, purchase and payment ID; security logs; optional preferences. Document each purpose, lawful basis (not blanket consent), retention, recipients and transfers. Article 13/14 privacy notices accessible pre-signup, linked in footer and account settings.
- PRIV-002 Privacy by default. No analytics, adtech, third-party trackers, marketing emails, profiling or external player initialization by default. Collect minimal profile data; never put user name/email in public repo paths or Git URLs; keep private grading and payment state in D1. Require explicit opt-in for optional processing. Do not bundle separate legal purposes into a single forced checkbox.
- PRIV-003 Rights portal. Provide authenticated privacy dashboard for access/download, correction, deletion requests, objection/restriction where applicable, portability where applicable, and account closure; provide unauthenticated contact method. A human/admin process must review exceptions such as legally required invoices/audits and third-party owner-managed GitHub submissions. Log status and deadlines, identity verification and fulfillment; do not promise instantaneous irreversible Git history erasure.
- PRIV-004 Retention. Define clear defaults with controller review, scheduled cleanup and export for sessions, upload reservations, grades, submissions, purchase records, consent receipts, notices, backups and security audit logs. Use time-based retention in configuration, not arbitrary infinite storage. Notify learners that course owner controls GitHub repository retention; establish an instructor contractual deletion procedure.
- PRIV-005 Security. Least privilege, encryption in transit, secrets isolation, DPIA screening for higher-risk processing, incident runbook, processors' DPAs, transfer mechanisms and subprocessors inventory; notify supervisory authority where required under Article 33 GDPR (without undue delay, where feasible within 72 hours when reportable), and affected subjects when Article 34 applies. Record incident awareness timestamp and decision rationale.
- PRIV-006 Age handling. Until age-specific legal review, use clear age eligibility; do not build behavioral ads, and require additional protections where minors may enroll, in line with GDPR/DSA rules. Age of digital consent differs by EU member state; do not hardcode a universal age.

## COOKIE requirements

- COOKIE-001 Strictly necessary cookies (e.g. `__Host-course_session`) may be set for the service explicitly requested without a cookie-consent banner. This is **not** a reason to omit a truthful Cookie Policy. Session cookie must be `Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/` with no `Domain`; rotate/revoke sessions. Avoid localStorage for auth tokens. Keep CSRF protections for mutation routes.
- COOKIE-002 If **any** non-essential cookie, local storage tracker, analytics, advertising SDK or device storage access is introduced, block it until valid granular prior permission; offer equally accessible Accept/Reject/Customize controls, no pre-ticked categories, easy withdrawal from persistent Cookie Settings, and versioned consent records. Non-essential features never load simply because a banner is dismissed.
- COOKIE-003 Third-party media. Display local thumbnail/placeholders for YouTube/Vimeo/external players by default, with a clear "Load external video" action and provider/privacy notice. No third-party player iframe, tracking pixels or preload before the legally appropriate permission. Provide direct link where preview requires external service. If a provider sets non-essential cookies, implement appropriate consent; a `nocookie` hostname alone is not proof of consentlessness. Honor refusal and allow re-consent per provider/category.
- COOKIE-004 Publish operator-verified Cookie Policy with each storage mechanism, host, purpose, category, approximate lifetime, recipients, consent/legal basis and change date; generate inventory from actual runtime cookies/SDKs, not imaginary statements. Integrate privacy policy, ToS and settings in footer and registration screens.
- COOKIE-005 Analytics-free baseline must work with **no cookie banner** (only necessary cookies). Do not deploy a decorative or coercive consent wall. If optional consent is needed, rejecting must not block unrelated course access.

## Contractual and operational tests

- CT-001 New visitor receives no optional tracker/cookie or video iframe; strictly necessary session only after authentication. CT-002 Reject vs Accept produces different network/storage behavior, accepts/withdraws persistent categories and stores minimal receipt. CT-003 Session cookie never readable in JS and origin/CSRF attack blocked. CT-004 DSAR export and deletion/restriction pathways tested with internal D1 and owner GitHub records. CT-005 Privacy/Terms/Cookie Policy links accessible without login and on mobile. CT-006 Processor incident and retention runbooks exist and are linked in admin.
