# Landing concept: Codex critique

Independent second opinion from Codex on the concept screenshots of 2026-11-21 (commit 1c8f531), run read-only with the brief in the prompt. Kept verbatim; the design lead resolves it together with the design review.

I’d hold this back from the owner. “Open the course. Close the other tabs.” is a good hook, and the first screen wastes it on a list of file formats before the reader understands what Lectale does.

## Ten changes, ranked

1. **Put the GitHub-to-course idea next to the headline.** In images 1 and 4, the explanation sits far to the right and starts above the headline. In image 5, it lands directly underneath, yet still leaves the reader wondering what kind of course they can open. Keep the headline and lead with “Open a course hosted on GitHub and read its lessons, PDFs and notebooks in one place.” Give “Free to read without an account” its own short line. On desktop, align the explanation with the top of the headline and keep it within a 380–400 px column. Rename the gold button “Open a course link” and take readers straight to the repository field shown in image 12.

2. **Make the product preview look like a reader someone is using.** In image 1, the 164 px gap between the contents column and lesson makes the two feel disconnected, and the lesson title is barely larger than the prose. In image 5, “Contents” looks tappable and the frame slices through a paragraph. Bring the desktop lesson 64–80 px closer to the sidebar, set its title at 26–28 px, and show the course name so readers know where they are. On mobile, use one compact row for the current lesson, then end the preview after a complete paragraph. The ribbon already shows where the learner is. Give the lesson itself enough hierarchy to explain what they’re reading.

3. **Give the middle of the page a faster rhythm.** In image 1, the formats section gives a 48 px heading and introductory paragraph to the left half, then stacks six text blocks on the right. Image 2 follows it with oversized step numbers and roughly 140 px of space after the action. The page spends a lot of height repeating the promise already made in the hero. Use “What opens in Lectale” at 42–44 px on desktop and 30–32 px on mobile, then let the six format labels carry the detail. Give those labels a consistent 22–24 px size and the descriptions about 17/26 px. Reduce the step numbers to 40–44 px and shorten step three to “Save a course or enrol,” with the account requirement in its description. Aim for 88–96 px of desktop section padding and 56–64 px on phones.

4. **Make the instructor section show the instructor’s actual next move.** In image 2, “Keep your course. Lectale presents it.” is followed by a generic “Create account” button. Image 12 already has the more specific action, “Connect a repository.” Bring that wording to the landing page and carry it through any sign-in step. In the diagram, label the two columns “Your repository” and “What learners see,” and use the same lesson names as the reader preview. In images 6 and 7, the diagram stacks, so replace the caption’s left/right directions with “Lectale builds this outline from your course files.”

5. **Art-direct the covers, then give the catalog room to behave like a catalog.** The histogram in images 2 and 10 works. The manuscript looks like a document icon enlarged to poster size, the scatterplot floats in a mostly empty frame, and the sixth cover looks like an asset waiting to load. Give the manuscript a distinctive initial and page crop, enlarge the scatterplot, and use consistent title insets and illustration placement. On the landing page, keep the portrait covers. In the catalog, image 10 puts 588 px of artwork ahead of each title and description. Use roughly 120 × 180 px thumbnails alongside the course details at desktop width so people can compare results without scrolling through a wall of covers.

6. **Finish the downloads section before anyone sees it.** Images 3 and 8 show `-- MB` on every native download, and the QR caption promises the Android app while the printed destination is a general releases page. Both make the download buttons feel provisional. Show verified filenames and sizes once they’re available, and omit size until there’s a value. Name the QR destination “Android release page” and use the same destination for the adjacent text link. On mobile, cut the large gaps between platform rows to about 32 px and keep each platform name, description, and full-width button together. The Web row should remain first.

7. **Design the 390 px catalog teaser as its own layout.** Image 7 leaves roughly 60 px between “Browse the catalog” and the first covers, then squeezes the metadata into two narrow columns. “Reading medieval manuscripts” takes three lines and pushes its description well below its neighbor. The third example seen on desktop disappears. Deliberately feature two examples on phones, bring the covers within 24–32 px of the catalog link, and keep the title, description, and language together in each card. Move the demo disclosure above the pair and drop the repeated “Example instructor” and “Example course” labels. The full catalog can carry all six.

8. **Close the page with a finished privacy and footer section.** In image 3, “No tracking” occupies a large standalone band for two sentences and a link. On mobile, it reads like another full product feature after a long downloads list. Keep the heading, statement, and privacy link, but bring them together in a compact block with about 64 px of vertical padding. The `{operator name}` placeholder in images 3 and 9 needs the actual operator before review. It’s the most visibly unfinished line on the page.

9. **Make the small interaction details carry their weight.** In images 5 and 9, several text links have little visible space around them. In image 11, “Show password” shares a 48 px field with the password itself. Give the account and footer links 44 px hit areas, and reserve a dedicated button area inside the password field so typed text can’t run underneath it. Use a visible 2 px gold focus ring on links, fields, and buttons. In image 10, keep the visible filter labels and make the result count announce changes as filters are applied. Check the small gray captions and outlined controls against their actual backgrounds in both themes.

10. **Give dark mode the same editorial hierarchy as light mode.** In image 4, the reader surface nearly merges into the page, while the gold CTA and ribbon are the strongest marks on screen. The muted chapter label and preview caption recede enough that the screenshot feels flatter than image 1. Add a subtle boundary around the reader surface, raise the contrast of the small gray text, and give the selected lesson a clearer surface behind its label. Keep the geometry consistent across themes and preserve gold for the primary action, ribbon, and focus.

## What should stay

- **The two-sentence headline.** It sounds like something a person would say after fighting with six course tabs.
- **Source Serif 4 for reading and IBM Plex Sans for controls.** The pairing feels most convincing inside the reader, where prose and navigation have distinct jobs.
- **The warm paper, graphite, and mulberry palette.** It gives Lectale a recognizable identity with very little decoration.
- **The gold bookmark ribbon.** It communicates reading position in a small space and ties the landing preview to the signed-in home.
- **The account-free reading promise.** Keep it visible next to the primary action, where it can answer a real question before someone clicks.
