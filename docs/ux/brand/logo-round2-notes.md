# Lectale logo · Round 2

Lectale turns a GitHub course repository into a place to read. The mark should feel at home beside a lesson title and make sense to a learner in any discipline. Round 1’s top-left mark made the bookmark visible in the shape of the page. Round 2 keeps that link to the learner’s place in Contents and tests how much of the page shape the mark needs.

## Twelve ideas

1. **Ribbon Cut.** Cut a wide swallowtail into the top margin of a tall page with a rounded outer shoulder.
2. **Ribbon Foot.** Extend the page’s spine below the baseline into a blunt swallowtail bookmark.
3. **Outer Notch.** Cut a swallowtail inward from the page’s fore-edge at the learner’s current line.
4. **Floating Ribbon.** Set a detached, single-colour swallowtail in an open page margin.
5. **Folded Corner.** Enlarge a turned page corner until its negative space resembles a folded ribbon.
6. **Line Break.** Give three reading lines one decisive shift in the middle to mark where reading resumes.
7. **Course Folios.** Use two broad paper shapes separated by a stepped gutter to suggest a course gathered into a sequence.
8. **Margin Brackets.** Frame an open reading column with two small printer’s corner pieces.
9. **Section Tabs.** Stack three plate-like blocks with one protruding edge to suggest a lesson index.
10. **Reading Window.** Open a rectangular aperture in an otherwise solid leaf to suggest a focused reading pane.
11. **Course Thread.** Link three rounded steps with a broad path from the first lesson to the next.
12. **Paragraph Return.** Use two type-like blocks connected by a right-angle turn to evoke the next paragraph.

## Shortlist

| Study | Reason for selection |
| --- | --- |
| 01 · Ribbon Cut | It preserves the strongest idea from round 1. A wider cut and an asymmetric outer shoulder give the bookmark more room at favicon size. |
| 02 · Ribbon Foot | It tests the same promise in the outer silhouette. The swallowtail can carry directly into the in-app bookmark. |
| 03 · Line Break | It uses the rhythm of reading to signal a place in the text, with no reliance on a book silhouette. |
| 04 · Course Folios | It suggests how Lectale gathers disparate course files into a reading sequence. The stepped gutter gives the two leaves a shared contour. |

The other page and ribbon ideas either lose too much of the reading cue or require fine details to carry it. The index, aperture, corners, and path skew toward navigation or document utilities.

Palette letters follow this round’s brief. **A** is cool Birch and cobalt. **B** is warm paper and mulberry. The token plan uses the opposite letters.

## Contrast

Ratios use WCAG relative luminance and the exact PNG palette colours. They describe the solid fills. Edge pixels are antialiased. The accent swap in dark mode is necessary. Reusing the light cobalt on the A dark canvas gives **2.19:1**. Reusing the light mulberry on the B dark canvas gives **2.15:1**. Both are below the 3:1 target.

The token plan leaves cool dark ink as a soft off-white. I selected `#E4E9EB` for the proof. The single-colour reverse proof on each study uses Birch against the daytime cobalt, also 7.24:1. For a mulberry accent surface, warm paper gives 7.16:1.

## Recommendation

**Use 01 · Ribbon Cut in B · Warm paper and mulberry.** The bookmark is recognisable in the page at 16 px and repeats a shape learners will use to find their place in Contents. The warm paper and muted accent suit long reading sessions, and the pale dark-mode mulberry keeps the full contour legible at 8.65:1. **02 · Ribbon Foot** is the runner-up. Its silhouette carries the swallowtail, with a slightly slower read at favicon size.

The four study boards show palette A for a direct comparison. The overview compares 01 and 02 in both palettes, with light and dark lockups and 24 px standalones. I developed each study and one revision with the built-in image tool, then traced the revised silhouettes onto a shared 64-unit grid for exact palette and size checks. The lockups use Lectale’s bundled IBM Plex Sans Medium.
